# Managing AI coding sessions with JustSessions

[Back to JustSessions](../../README.md) · [Session storage and privacy](session-storage.md)

Use the sidebar to find a conversation, read its preview, and resume it in its original CLI. These instructions cover the controls, notifications, SSH setup, terminal persistence, cleanup, and appearance settings.

## Find any session

- All Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity CLI, Kiro CLI, OpenCode, and Pi sessions in one sidebar, grouped by machine (this Mac, then each SSH host), then by project folder, sorted by recent activity. A Claude Code session moved with `/cd` is listed under the folder it moved to.
- Search projects by name or path, and sessions by title, session ID, message text, or a bookmark's label or tag: click the magnifying glass at the top of the sidebar. Its field opens across the top line; **Escape** or its **×** clears and closes it, and an empty field closes once you click elsewhere.
- Message text is what the reader shows: your messages, the CLI's replies, and tool call summaries, ignoring case and accents. A session whose messages match stays under its project with the matching line beneath its title; click it to read the conversation at that message, with Find open on your search. The first search reads each session once, most recent first, and the top of the list shows how many are done; later searches read again only sessions that changed. Text past a message's preview limit is not searched. See [message search](session-storage.md#message-search) for what is kept on your Mac.
- Choose **Projects** to browse the library or **Open tabs** to switch between this window's open terminals. Each view keeps its search and scroll position when switching; each window remembers the selected view.
- In **Projects**, use the filter menu beside the switch to choose **Last 7 days** or one CLI. **Running** lists only sessions whose CLI runs, in a tab of any window or in tmux after its tab closed, on this Mac or on an SSH host as of the host's last refresh; a CLI started outside JustSessions is not counted. **Waiting for you** lists only sessions whose CLI waits on you: stopped for your answer, or done with a turn you haven't seen. Only Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity, Pi, and OpenCode on this Mac, and Claude Code, Codex, Pi, and OpenCode in a tab on an SSH host, tell when they wait, so it never lists other CLIs. Archived sessions stay hidden until you turn on **Show archived**. These filters do not hide open tabs. The menu lists only CLIs that are installed or have sessions. Refresh each machine using its own heading's refresh button.
- To stop seeing a CLI you have installed but don't use, untick it under **Show sessions from** in **Settings > General**. Its sessions leave **Projects**, search, and the filter menu, along with projects only it worked in, and **New session** stops offering it, in every window. Nothing is deleted: its open tabs stay, and ticking it again lists its sessions at once.
- An expanded project lists its 10 most recent sessions after its pinned and new ones. Click **Show 20 more** at the end of the list for more; collapsing the project goes back to 10. A search, **Running**, or **Waiting for you** lists every match, and a session you open from elsewhere, such as Back or a notification, is listed even when it is older.
- Pin projects and sessions to keep them at the top, in the order you put them: a new pin goes last, and activity never moves one. Drag a pinned project or session to reorder it, or drag another up among the pinned ones to pin it there. Projects move among their host's projects, sessions among their project's; the rest stay sorted by recent activity. Let go off the list and nothing moves. Rename any session locally without touching the CLI's own title.
- Choose **Archive session** from a session's **⋯** or right-click menu, or **Archive … sessions** from a multi-selection's, to hide sessions you're done with from **Projects**. Nothing is deleted: the session's file, open tab, name, and pin stay, and search leaves it out too. Turn on **Show archived** in the filter menu to list archived sessions with the others, each with an archive box after its title, and choose **Unarchive session** to bring one back. Resuming an archived session, from its menu, a double-click, dropping its row on the terminals, or **Move to**, also brings it back; tabs reopened at launch don't. A project whose sessions are all archived stays listed, empty.
- Subagents' sessions are hidden by default. Turn on **Show subagents under the sessions that started them** in **Settings > General**, and a session whose subagents ran sessions of their own shows a chevron before its icon. Click it to list them under the session, newest first; they stay hidden until you do. Click one to read it in the preview. A subagent's session is only read: it has no Resume, Branch, Rename, Pin, or Delete. Deleting a Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, or Pi session deletes its subagents' sessions too, and the confirmation says how many; an Antigravity session's stay on disk. [Which subagents are listed](session-storage.md#session-locations).
- A Claude Code or Codex session's header in the reader, and its tab's hover card, show the model it used last, as the CLI records it, such as `claude-opus-4-8`, or `gpt-5.5 · medium` with Codex's reasoning effort. A subagent's row shows its model shortened, such as `haiku-4-5`, since a subagent often runs on another model than its session. Other CLIs don't show a model yet.
- Hide the sidebar to give the terminal or preview the whole window: click the sidebar button next to the window's close, minimize, and zoom buttons, choose **View → Hide Sidebar**, or press **⌘B**. Each window keeps its own choice, and the sidebar comes back with the same projects expanded.
- Go back to the tab or session you were looking at, as in Chrome: click the back arrow after the sidebar button, choose **Go → Back**, or press **⌘[**; the forward arrow, **Go → Forward**, or **⌘]** returns. Right-click either arrow to pick from the tabs and sessions it goes through. Each window keeps its own history while it is open. Back skips tabs that have closed and sessions the sidebar's search or filters hide. **⌘←** and **⌘→** stay with the terminal, where they move the cursor to the start and end of the line.

## Preview before you resume

- Read a Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity, Kiro CLI, OpenCode, or Pi conversation without starting the CLI. Your messages and the agent's replies are shown; runs of tool calls collapse into one expandable row. Images you attached, and images a tool returned such as screenshots, are shown in place for Claude Code, Codex, and Pi, scaled to the reading column.
- The new-window button at the end of the preview's reading toolbar opens a separate, read-only window at your current position without changing the selected terminal. Opening it again brings the existing window for that session forward; different sessions can have separate reading windows.
- Large conversations load in pages, starting with the latest messages or your saved reading position. Nearby history loads automatically as the viewport approaches either end, including while scrolling inside a long message or after restoring a position near an edge. **Load earlier messages** / **Load later messages** remain available for manual loading and retries. The reader keeps three pages, and a few more while short ones, such as a few screenshots each, are all near the viewport. It reloads distant pages when you return to them; loading history preserves your position within the message, even if you keep scrolling during the load. The first/latest buttons can jump beyond the currently loaded pages.
- Use **A− / A+**, or **⌘+ / ⌘−**, to adjust text size from 12 to 22 points; **⌘0** returns it to 15. The width button switches between a readable column and the full window width. The preview and every reading window share the text size and the width, which are remembered after you quit. The up/down buttons go to the first or latest available message. Markdown formatting, tables, and code blocks remain readable; wide tables and code blocks scroll horizontally, and code blocks have a **Copy** button.
- Switching sessions restores your reading position while the workspace window stays open, including a position within a long message. Reading positions are not saved across app restarts. A separate reading window keeps its own position until it closes.
- Press **⌘F**, or click the magnifying glass in the reading toolbar, to search the displayed conversation. Matches ignore case and accents and include message text, code, table cells, and tool summaries. Use **Return / ⌘G** for the next match, **⇧Return / ⇧⌘G** for the previous one, or the arrow buttons; navigation wraps at either end. **Escape** closes search. The selected match is highlighted and brought into view, and a matching tool summary opens its disclosure. Each preview or reading window has its own search. Search covers the currently loaded pages; unloaded pages and text beyond a message's preview limit are not searched. Automatic page loading pauses while Find is open; the page buttons remain available. The shortcuts apply to the reader while it is visible and leave active CLI terminals alone.
- Bookmark messages to come back to them, like marks in vim: press **⌘D** (**Bookmarks → Add or Remove Bookmark**) to bookmark the message you're reading, or right-click a message and choose **Add bookmark**. Each bookmark takes the lowest free letter, a to z, shown beside its message; a conversation holds 26. ⌘D on a bookmarked message, or **Remove bookmark** in its right-click menu, removes it. **⌘'** and **⇧⌘'** go to the next and previous bookmark, wrapping at either end and loading history as needed; the bookmark button in the reading toolbar lists them with each message's first words and offers **Remove all bookmarks…**. The message you're reading is the one you last jumped to, until you scroll, or else the first message at the top of the reader, below its toolbar. A ring in the margin beside it, with a line down the rest of the message, shows which message ⌘D will bookmark as you scroll. Only your messages and the agent's replies can be bookmarked. Bookmarks are kept after you quit, shown in every window, and work in SSH and subagent sessions; deleting the session removes them. If a bookmarked message is no longer in the conversation, as after leaving a Pi branch with `/tree`, the bookmark stays, and going to it shows where the message was. In a tab linked to a session, **⌘D** bookmarks the session's latest message, yours or the agent's, or removes its bookmark, and a notice in the terminal's corner names its letter; **⌘'** and **⇧⌘'** open the session's reading window at the next or previous bookmark and leave the tab selected. On an SSH host they use the app's copy of the session, which can be a few minutes behind. The shortcuts never reach the CLI.
- Give a bookmark a label and tags to find it again: press **⇧⌘D** (**Bookmarks → Edit Bookmark…**), choose **Edit bookmark…** in the reading toolbar's bookmark button or in a bookmarked message's right-click menu, or right-click a letter on the conversation map. ⇧⌘D bookmarks the message first if it has no bookmark, and never removes one; in a tab it acts on the session's latest message, as ⌘D does. Type a label of up to 80 characters and tags such as `#idea #todo`, separated by spaces or commas; tags you've used before are offered below the field. Point at a labelled or tagged letter beside its message, or at its letter on the map, to see them; the bookmark button lists a bookmark by its label and tags.
- Find bookmarks across sessions: **Bookmarks → Show All Bookmarks…** (**⌥⌘B**) lists every session's bookmarks, newest session first. Search their labels, tags, and messages, or a session's title; type `#tag` to find a tag, or click tags above the list to show only bookmarks with all of them. Choose a bookmark to open its session's reading window there; right-click one to edit or remove it. The sidebar's search also finds bookmarks by label and tag: a matching session shows the bookmark under its title, and clicking the session opens the reader at it.

## Resume, branch, and start sessions

- Resume a session in its native CLI inside an embedded terminal: double-click it, use **Resume**, or choose **Resume** from its **⋯** or right-click menu. Pointing at a project or a session shows its **⋯** button, which opens the same menu as a right-click. While a tab runs the session, clicking it shows that tab, and the menu leaves out **Resume**. While its CLI runs, in a tab of any window or in tmux with no tab open, the menu offers **End session**, which stops the CLI and closes its tab at once, without asking; you can resume the session later. With several sessions selected (⌘-click, ⇧-click), right-click one of them, or use the bar at the bottom of the sidebar, for **End … sessions**, which ends those whose CLI runs. To close just the tab, close it from the tab bar or **Open tabs**, which asks whether to keep the CLI running. A tab whose CLI ended, as when an SSH connection drops or the CLI quits, stays open showing how it ended; resuming its session starts it again in that tab, in its place and any split, rather than in a second tab. Closing an ended tab, a plain terminal's included, doesn't ask first, since nothing is left to stop; an SSH tab whose CLI or shell may still run in tmux on the host still asks.
- If a session's project folder on this Mac was moved, renamed, or deleted after the session ran, **Resume** and **Branch** stay off, and the preview says where the folder was. Move the folder back there to resume the session; the preview checks again when you come back to JustSessions. On an SSH host the folder isn't checked first; the terminal reports it when the CLI starts.
- Branch (fork) a Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, or Pi conversation into a new session. This forks the conversation, not a Git branch.
- Move a Claude Code session to another folder: choose **Move to** from its **⋯** or right-click menu, or the preview's **More** menu, then another project on the same machine, or **Choose folder…** on this Mac. The session resumes in a tab with Claude Code's `/cd` as its first message, so Claude Code moves the session itself and tells it about the new folder. In a folder the session hasn't worked in, Claude Code asks first in the tab; **No, stay put** leaves the session where it was. The sidebar lists the session under its new folder after the next refresh. The tab stays with its first project's tabs until it closes. **Move to** is off while the session's CLI runs; type `/cd` in its tab instead. On an SSH host, add a new folder as a project first, from the host's **+**.
- Kiro CLI supports a fork at an earlier turn with `/rewind` inside the resumed session. It does not currently expose a direct branch launch flag, so JustSessions keeps its Branch action unavailable. See [Kiro's rewind documentation](https://kiro.dev/docs/cli/reference/slash-commands/#rewind).
- Start a new session in any project folder, on this Mac or an SSH host, with any supported CLI installed on that machine. It appears in the sidebar right away. A typed folder is checked on its host first. If there is no such folder, the sheet says so and **Create folder and start** creates it, with any missing parent folders; editing the path or changing the host withdraws the offer. On this Mac, `~` and a relative path start from your home folder, as on an SSH host.
- Change how a CLI starts in the **New session** sheet's **Start command**, for any supported CLI. The field is locked so a stray keystroke can't change it: click **Edit**, type the command, then click **Save** or press Return; **Cancel** keeps the saved one. **Start session** waits until you save or cancel. Saving checks the command first and keeps it only if its program is there: on this Mac on the PATH JustSessions gives CLIs, on an SSH host in its login shell. A missing program, an unclosed quote, or a shell operator such as `;`, `&&`, `|`, or `#` is reported under the field instead. Flags aren't checked; only the CLI knows its own. Use a wrapper with flags of your own, for example: `~/.toolbox/bin/claude --aws-profile "dev" --dangerously-skip-permissions`, `codex --dangerously-bypass-approvals-and-sandbox`, or `agy --dangerously-skip-permissions`. Write it as you would in a shell; quotes, `~`, and `NAME=value` in front work, a shell alias does not. JustSessions adds its own arguments after it, such as the session to resume. Kiro CLI's options belong to its `chat` subcommand, so its command starts with `kiro-cli chat`, as in `kiro-cli chat --trust-all-tools`, and JustSessions adds no second `chat`. The empty field shows the command JustSessions uses otherwise. The command is kept for that CLI on that host, so resuming and branching its sessions there use it too, also from the sidebar. To go back to the default, click **Edit**, then **Use default** or clear the field, then **Save**; like any edit, it changes nothing until you save.
- Add a project folder before it has any sessions: point at the host's heading, such as **This Mac**, and click its **+** (choose **Add project…** if it opens a menu), or choose **Add project…** from the heading's right-click menu. On this Mac you pick the folder, or make one with **New Folder**; for an SSH host you type its path, which is checked on the host. If the host has no such folder, the sheet says so and **Create folder and add** creates it there, with any missing parent folders. The project is listed under the host, selected, and its own **+** menu starts sessions in it. Adding an archived folder restores it.
- For a session you need only once, tick **Delete the session when its tab closes** in the **New session** sheet. The sheet says where the session goes: Claude Code, Antigravity, and Pi sessions on this Mac move to the Trash; Codex, Kiro CLI, and OpenCode sessions, and every session on an SSH host, are deleted for good. Its tab and sidebar row show an hourglass. Closing the tab ends the CLI and deletes the session without asking. Closing the window or quitting keeps it until you close its tab. To keep it after all, choose **Keep session** from the tab's right-click menu. If you close the tab while its sidebar row is still italic, before the tab has found its session, nothing is deleted; if the CLI moves to another session, for example with `/clear` or `/resume`, both sessions are kept.
- Open a plain terminal in a project folder: choose **Terminal** from the project's **+** menu or in the **New session** sheet, or choose **New terminal** from the project's **⋯** or right-click menu. It runs your login shell, on this Mac or the project's SSH host. It is not a session: it appears in **Open tabs**, not the saved session library, and sends no notifications. Where the host has tmux, the shell runs in tmux, so it keeps running when you quit, an update installs, or an SSH connection drops, and its tab attaches to it again when it reopens or reconnects. Closing its tab ends the shell, as do closing one of several windows and quitting with **Reopen tabs** off. On an SSH host with tmux older than 3.7, the shell keeps as many lines as the host's tmux `history-limit` allows. Tick **Don't ask again** in its close dialog to close later terminals without asking; change it back in **Settings → General → Tabs**.
- Keep several terminal tabs open. Select a session in the sidebar to read its preview; a session whose CLI is running opens on its terminal instead. Select a terminal tab to return to its CLI without stopping it.
- With several windows open, a session runs in one tab across them. Resuming it in another window, or clicking a notification about it, brings forward the window with its tab and selects it. Clicking the session there shows its preview, which says it is open in another window and offers **Show**; its **⋯** menu offers **Show in Other Window** in place of **Resume**. Its row and project show what that tab's CLI is doing. Once that tab or window closes, or its CLI ends, the session opens in any window. To move the tab instead, click **Move Here** in the preview or choose **Move Tab to This Window** from the **⋯** menu: the tab closes in its window and the session reopens in yours, its CLI still running in tmux. The move is offered only when tmux runs the CLI; on an SSH host, the host confirms it first, and without tmux the tab stays where it is.
- The sidebar's **Open tabs** view uses the full list area and follows tab bar order, grouped by project like the tab bar. Each group's heading shows the project in its tab group's color, its remote host when applicable, and its tab count; each row shows its title and CLI status. Pointing at a heading shows its project's **⋯** and **+**, as in **Projects**: **+** starts a session or terminal in the folder, and **⋯** or a right-click opens the menu of the group's label in the tab bar. Renaming, pinning, and archiving the project stay in **Projects**. Click a heading to collapse its group, as with a project; a collapsed group still shows when a CLI is waiting on you, and selecting one of its tabs from the tab bar expands it. Search by title, project name or path, or SSH host; a search expands every group, and **Close all tabs in group…** closes only the tabs the search lists. Click a row to show its terminal, or right-click it for its tab's menu, as in the tab bar. To close several tabs at once, ⌘-click rows to add or remove them, or Shift-click to select a range, even across groups; then click **Close … tabs…** at the bottom of the sidebar, or choose it from a selected row's right-click menu. It asks once for them all, as **Close all tabs in group…** does. Click a row, empty space, or press Escape to cancel the selection. Right-click the empty space below the groups for **New session…**, **Collapse all groups**, and **Expand all groups**. Switching sidebar views does not change the selected terminal.
- Tabs group by project, like tab groups in a browser: a new tab opens next to its project's other tabs, behind a label in the project's color. Click the label to collapse or expand the group. A collapsed group shows only its label, with how many tabs it hides and the most pressing status among them. Collapsing the group of the tab you're on switches to the nearest tab still in sight; selecting a hidden tab, by shortcut or from the sidebar, expands its group. Drag a tab to reorder it among its group's tabs; it stays in its project's group, and a split's two tabs move together. Drag a group's label to move the whole group. Drag a tab out of the bar to move it into a new window, as in Chrome, or onto another window's tab bar to move it there; drag a group's label out of the bar to move the whole group the same way; a tab that can show in a split view stays put while over the terminals, and moves into a new window only past them; its terminal keeps running, and a split's two tabs go together. A window closes once its last tab leaves. The tab bar's empty space works like a title bar: drag it to move the window, or double-click it to zoom or minimize the window, as set in System Settings. The order is kept when tabs reopen after you quit.
- Right-click a group's label for **New session**, with one of the CLIs installed on the group's host, or **New terminal**, in the group's project folder; the new tab opens at the end of the group and is selected. The menu also opens the folder in Finder or an editor, copies its path, collapses or expands the group, offers **Collapse all groups** and **Expand all groups**, and ends with **Close all tabs in group…**. That asks once for all the group's tabs, and only what closing each tab would ask: **Keep running** or **End sessions** while a CLI can keep running in tmux, otherwise whether to stop the CLIs and shells. Choices saved with **Don't ask again** answer only when no tab in the group would otherwise ask; each CLI follows its own host's choice.
- Right-click a session's tab, or its ×, for **Rename**, **Copy conversation**, **Export conversation**, **Copy session ID**, **Reveal session file in Finder** for a session on this Mac, the split view items, and **Close Tab…**, which asks whether to keep the CLI running. While its CLI runs, **Close and end session** closes the tab and stops the CLI at once, without asking, as **End session** does from the session's row; you can resume the session later. **Close and delete session…** closes the tab, ends the CLI, and deletes the session after you confirm. Pin a session from its row in **Projects**; the tab doesn't pin it. A plain terminal's tab has the split view items and **Close terminal…**.
- Right-click the tab bar's empty space for **New session…**, which opens the **New session** sheet as ⌘N does, **Collapse all groups**, and **Expand all groups**. Collapsing every group keeps the tab you're on showing behind its group's label. A split across two projects never collapses.
- As in Chrome, tabs narrow together to fit the tab bar as more open. A title too long for its tab fades out at the tab's edge; point at the tab to see it in full. A narrow tab shows its × only when selected or under the pointer. Once tabs are as narrow as they get, the tab bar scrolls sideways.
- Show two tabs side by side in a split view, as in Chrome, or one above the other. Right-click another tab and choose **New split view with current tab** or **New split view below current tab**, or right-click the tab you're on, or its terminal, and choose **Split right** or **Split down**: each lists a new session of each CLI and **Terminal** in the tab's project, then your other tabs. Or press **⌘⌥N**, as in Chrome: the tab you're on moves to the left, and the right view lists this window's other tabs that aren't showing or in a split, the one you used last first. Click one, or pick it with **↑**, **↓**, and **Return**, to show it there; with no other tab to list, **New Session…** starts one there. Until you pick, the tab bar shows the list's half of the split as **Choose a tab**; its ×, or ⌘W while the list has the keyboard, closes the list and leaves your tab full width. The other tab moves next to yours, into your tab's group, and the tab further left in the bar shows on the left. A split's two tabs join into one tab half as wide each; while either is selected, both look selected and both terminals show, each in a rounded outline with a small toolbar in its corner. Click a pane, or its toolbar, to type in it, and drag the handle between the panes to resize them; near the middle, they snap even. Double-click the handle to reverse the views. Several splits can be open at once, each tab in one at most, and each keeps its own sizes. While a split shows, right-click a tab outside it and choose **Move tab into split view** to swap it for the left or right view. Right-click either tab of a split and use **Arrange split view** to separate the views, close the left or right view, reverse them, or stack them with **Stack views** (**Place views side by side** puts them back); for the shown split, the **Tabs** menu offers the same as **Separate Views**, **Close Left View**, **Close Right View**, **Reverse Views**, and **Stack Views**. Stacked, the first tab in the bar shows on top, and the entries say top and bottom. Drag a view by its toolbar to another edge of the terminals to move it there, side by side or stacked, or to the middle to separate the split. A separated tab from another project goes back to its project's group. Or drag a tab onto the terminals, as in Claude Code: where it would show lights up with its title. Let go near the left or right edge to show it beside the tab you're on, near the top or bottom edge to show it above or below, or in the middle to show it on its own, full width; the tab you were on stays open. While a split shows, it takes the place of the view you let go on. This works from the tab bar or another window, one tab at a time; a split's two tabs move together, so dragging them opens a new window instead. A session or tab dragged from the sidebar, in **Projects** or **Open tabs**, drops the same way; a session with no tab open is resumed in a new tab there. With no tab shown, the whole area lights up, and letting go resumes the session, or shows its tab, full width. A session's right-click menu offers the same as **Open in split on the right** and **Open in split below**, or, while a split shows, **Open in left view** and **Open in right view** (top and bottom when stacked). Close a view with the × on its toolbar or its tab, as you close any tab; the other tab stays open, full width. Splits aren't restored when tabs reopen after you quit: their tabs reopen on their own, each in its project's group.
- Drag files from Finder onto a terminal tab to type their paths, as in Terminal.app. Claude Code and Codex attach a dropped image to your prompt; other CLIs and a shell get the path. In a split view, the pane you drop on takes the keyboard. On a tab on an SSH host, the files are first copied to `~/.cache/justsessions/dropped-files` on the host, and the paths typed are the copies' there; a copy that takes a moment shows its progress, with **Cancel**, in the tab's corner. Folders aren't copied. Copies more than a week old are removed the next time you drop files on that host.
- Copy a screenshot or an image, then press **⌘V** in a Claude Code tab, or choose **Paste** from the Edit menu or a right-click, to attach it, as Claude Code's own Ctrl-V does. On a tab on an SSH host, the image is copied to the host as a dropped file is, with the same progress and **Cancel**, and its path there is typed, so the CLI attaches it as when you drop it. With text on the clipboard as well, as when you copy a file in Finder, Paste types the text.
- Edit the line you're typing in a terminal as in a text field: **⌘←** and **⌘→** go to its start and end, and **⌘⌫** deletes back to its start (zsh deletes the whole line). **⌥←** and **⌥→** move a word, and **⌥⌫** deletes one. In Claude Code and shells, **⌘Z** undoes your last edit; Codex, OpenCode, and Pi don't undo with it. The terminal sends these keys as the ones CLIs and shells read for the same edits, such as Ctrl-A and Ctrl-E.
- Press **⇧Return** while typing in Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, or Pi to add a line to your prompt instead of sending it, on this Mac and on an SSH host with tmux. The tab tells tmux the key was Shift-Return, and sets up the host's tmux to pass it on, so no tmux or CLI configuration is needed there.
- Press **⌘A** while typing in Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, Pi, or Antigravity to select only the text in its prompt, as in a text field. **⌘C** copies it, **⌫** deletes it, typing or **⌘V** replaces it, and **←** or **→** go to its start or end. Press **⌘A** again to select all of the terminal. Any other key goes to the CLI as usual, and a click ends the selection. Copy takes what the prompt shows, so text the CLI hides, such as Claude Code's `[Pasted text …]`, isn't copied. In Kiro CLI, in a plain terminal, and when the prompt is empty, ⌘A selects all of the terminal.
- Press **⌘F** in a terminal to search what it printed, ignoring case. The newest match is selected as you type; **Return / ⌘G** goes to older matches and **⇧Return / ⇧⌘G** to newer ones, as do the arrow buttons, wrapping at either end. **Escape** closes the bar while its field has the keyboard; otherwise Escape goes to the terminal. A SwiftTerm tab keeps the last 10,000 lines and shows how many match; a Ghostty tab shows no count. A plain terminal in tmux searches its shell's history with tmux's own search, which highlights every match, and shows how many match with either engine. A tab linked to a session searches its conversation instead, since the CLI's screen in tmux keeps no scrollback: ⌘F opens it in a reading window with Find showing, and the selected tab stays. ⌘D, ⌘', and ⇧⌘' work there too, on the session's bookmarks.
- ⌘-click a link in a terminal to open it: a web address opens in your browser, and a file path, such as `Sources/App/main.swift:42`, opens in the app macOS uses for that kind of file. A relative path is in the tab's project folder, and the line number is left out. To open files elsewhere, set **Settings → General → Tabs → When you ⌘-click a file**: **Ask each time** shows the right-click menu's choices for the file (below), and an installed editor opens code and other text files there, at the printed line in VS Code and editors built on it; images, PDFs, and other files still open in their own app, and so does every file if you remove that editor. Apps, scripts Terminal would run, and other executables don't open. In a tab on an SSH host only web addresses open, since its paths are on the host. Links a CLI adds to its text, which show other words than the address, open the same way, with either terminal engine. A link a CLI breaks over two or more rows opens whole, from any of its rows.
- Right-click a link for more. A web address offers **Open Link** and **Copy Link**. A file on this Mac offers **Open**, **Open in** an installed editor, **Reveal in Finder**, and **Copy Path**; VS Code, and editors built on it that can open SSH projects, open it at the printed line; other editors open it at the top. A folder offers **Open in Finder**, **Open in**, and **Copy Path**. A file that opening would run offers no **Open**, and a path in a tab on an SSH host offers only **Copy Path**.
- Right-click a terminal for **Copy**, **Paste**, and **Select All**, followed by its tab's right-click menu, from **Rename** to its closing items (**Close terminal…** for a plain terminal). In a split view, the pane you right-click takes the keyboard, so what you paste and type next goes to it.
- The app includes tmux for CLI sessions and plain terminals on this Mac. Direct `swift run` builds use an installed tmux 3.3 or later. Closing a tab with **Keep running**, quitting the app, or installing an update leaves its CLI running. Tick **Don't ask again on this Mac** (or on the SSH host's name) in the close dialog to close that host's later tabs the same way without asking. Each host keeps its own choice; **Settings → General → Tabs** lists them and can change any back. Select the session to reattach, or right-click it and choose **End session** to stop it. If a CLI on this Mac quits with an error, such as an unknown flag in its start command, its tab keeps what it printed, and the bar above the terminal shows the CLI's own exit status. Resume the session to start it again in that tab, or close the tab.
- Claude Code tabs on SSH hosts also pick up `/rename` names and follow `/clear` through the host's tmux relay, without waiting for a refresh. Names set in JustSessions still win. A CLI already running in tmux before this support was added must be ended and resumed once to start the new relay; closing its tab with **Keep running** and reattaching keeps the old relay.
- Pick up names set with `/rename` in Claude Code or `/name` in Pi, and names Codex gives its threads, within about a second while their tab is open on this Mac. After `/clear`, the tab follows Claude Code to the session it starts, which keeps the name. A Codex tab on this Mac likewise follows Codex to the thread `/new`, `/clear`, `/resume`, or `/fork` moves it to; a thread `/new` starts shows up once you send its first prompt. Antigravity, Pi, and OpenCode tabs on this Mac follow their CLI too when you switch it to another session, such as with `/new` in Pi or OpenCode or by resuming another one. Pi (0.65 or later) and OpenCode tell the app their session through a small extension the app starts them with; an OpenCode that runs with an `OPENCODE_TUI_CONFIG` of your own is not followed.
- See what each CLI is doing on its sidebar row, its project, and its tab: a turning arc while it works, an amber mark while it waits on your answer, a green dot while it waits for your next prompt, and a hollow circle once it ended in a tab still open. A CLI that finished its turn while its tab was off screen, or while the app was in the background, shows a blue dot instead of the green one, until you select its tab or show it in a split; the notification about it may be gone by then, but the dot stays while the CLI runs. A session with no running CLI shows how long ago it was active instead. Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity, Pi, OpenCode, and Kiro CLI on this Mac tell what they are doing, also while they run in tmux with no tab open, and so do they on an SSH host, in a tab or, every few seconds, in tmux with no tab open; elsewhere the green dot only says the CLI runs. On a host they need tmux, and Antigravity also needs `python3`.

## Notifications

JustSessions posts a macOS notification when a Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity, Pi, OpenCode, or Kiro CLI session finishes its turn, on this Mac or an SSH host. They also notify when they stop in the middle of a turn to wait on you, such as at a permission prompt. Click the notification to open the session: its tab, or a new tab that reattaches to its CLI in tmux.

- Sessions running in tmux with no tab open notify too, on this Mac and on an SSH host.
- A session whose tab is selected while JustSessions is in front sends none; you already see it.
- One that finished its turn out of sight keeps a blue dot on its row and tab after the notification is gone; see the status list above.
- macOS asks for permission the first time there is something to notify about.
- Turn either kind off in **Settings > General**. Banners, sounds, and Do Not Disturb follow **System Settings > Notifications**. **Settings > Permissions** shows whether macOS allows them.
- JustSessions starts Codex so that its terminal title says what it is doing, on this Mac and on SSH hosts. Codex 0.128 or later also says there when it waits for your approval or answer. With an older Codex, and for a Codex running in tmux on this Mac with no tab open, a wait shows as still working and sends no notification. Codex started this way runs without its shared background server, as Codex does with any command-line override. This was checked with Codex CLI 0.160.0 on an SSH host.
- Pi and OpenCode tell what they are doing through a small extension JustSessions starts them with. Pi needs version 0.80.4 or later, and 0.84.4 or later to tell when an extension's prompt, such as a permission gate, waits on you; Pi itself asks no permission. OpenCode tells nothing when you set `OPENCODE_TUI_CONFIG` yourself. On an SSH host, JustSessions first writes the extension to `~/.cache/justsessions/live-session-reporting` there.
- Antigravity CLI tells what it is doing in the conversation database it writes as it goes. A command waiting for your approval shows as waiting on you, without the CLI's own words for it. This was checked with Antigravity CLI 1.3.3 on this Mac and 1.3.6 on Linux; the database's format is not documented, so a later version may change what the app reads. On an SSH host, JustSessions reads the database with the host's `python3`.
- Kiro CLI tells what it is doing in the turn markers it keeps while a turn runs, which it takes away when the turn ends or you cancel it with Ctrl-C or Esc. Kiro CLI 2.31.1 keeps them; 2.16.2 doesn't, so with it a tab only says Kiro runs (run `kiro-cli update`). Kiro marks nothing while it asks for permission, so that shows as working, and it sends no notification of that kind. The markers' format is not documented, so a later version may change what the app reads.

The bell at the top of the sidebar lists the sessions **Waiting for you** finds, in every window, the most recent wait first; a blue dot marks it while the list isn't empty. Each row shows the CLI, the session, its project, why it waits, and for how long. A session whose terminal shows in its window is left out. One stopped for your answer stays listed until you answer it; one that finished its turn leaves once you look at it. Click a row to show the session as its notification would; a CLI in tmux with no tab open reattaches in the bell's window. The bell lists sessions whatever you turn off in **Settings > General** or **System Settings**.

## SSH hosts

The host runs Linux or macOS and needs `rsync` plus the CLI you want to run installed. It can log you in with your SSH keys or agent, or ask for a password, a key passphrase, or a second factor such as a one-time code; see [hosts that ask for a password or code](#hosts-that-ask-for-a-password-or-code). Antigravity and OpenCode hosts also need `python3`; deleting an Antigravity session there needs `lsof`. The app reads each CLI's history where the host's login shell points that CLI, as it does on this Mac: it follows `CLAUDE_CONFIG_DIR`, `CODEX_HOME`, `KIRO_HOME`, `PI_CODING_AGENT_SESSION_DIR`, `PI_CODING_AGENT_DIR`, and Pi's `sessionDir` setting, and finds OpenCode's database as OpenCode does. A folder setting is followed when it is an absolute path or starts with `~/`; otherwise the CLI's standard folder is read. Antigravity's history is always read from `~/.gemini/antigravity-cli`. Deleting a session on the host removes it from the folder it was copied from.

- Click **Add SSH host…** at the bottom left of the sidebar and enter a host from `~/.ssh/config` or `user@hostname`. Its Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity, Kiro CLI, OpenCode, and Pi sessions are listed under its own heading, below this Mac's.
- A pasted `ssh devbox` adds `devbox`. A port, as in `devbox:2222` or `ssh -p 2222 devbox`, can't be kept with the host, so the sheet shows the lines to add to `~/.ssh/config` instead. Other options, such as a key or a jump host, also go there.
- Under the field, the sheet shows the account and address an alias connects to. A host that connects to the same account and address as a listed one, through the same jump host or proxy, can't be added again, since it would list the same sessions twice.
- Before adding the host, the app logs in to it the way its background commands do. If that fails, the sheet says why, for example that the host can't be found. Fix it and click **Try again**, or click **Add anyway** for a host that is only offline for now. A host that asks for a password or a code, or whose host key this Mac hasn't seen, offers **Connect…**, which logs in and then adds it.
- Windows hosts aren't supported. The sheet, or the heading of a Windows host already listed, says so. To use a Windows PC, run your CLIs in WSL there and connect to WSL over SSH, for example to an SSH server running inside WSL. JustSessions then sees a Linux host, and lists only the sessions of CLIs that run in WSL. We haven't tested this setup.
- The app's background connections ignore `RemoteCommand`, `RequestTTY`, and port forwards in `~/.ssh/config`, so an alias set up for logging in by hand still works. Tabs ignore only `RemoteCommand` and keep your port forwards.
- `ssh` runs with the environment your login shell sets up, as in Terminal, even when JustSessions is opened from the Dock or Finder. That includes an `SSH_AUTH_SOCK` for an agent such as 1Password or Secretive, and the `PATH` a `ProxyCommand` tool needs. The app reads it once at launch, so quit and reopen JustSessions after changing your shell's startup files.
- A refresh's background connections share one login, which closes when that refresh ends, so a key that asks for Touch ID or a tap asks once. If `~/.ssh/config` supplies its own control path for the host or turns connection sharing off, the app keeps that policy. Each tab keeps a connection of its own, except on a host logged in to with **Connect…**.
- Resume, branch, start, and delete sessions on the host over SSH, just like on this Mac. SSH hosts have no Trash, so deleting a session there is permanent; for Pi, the folder beside the session file goes too.
- Refresh updates every host at once. A host that can't be reached shows the error on its heading; the others still list.
- Hosts also refresh on their own while a JustSessions window shows on screen: this Mac every minute, each SSH host every 5 minutes, and both when you come back to the app. A host whose refresh failed is tried again after 5 minutes, then less often, up to once an hour; a host that needs **Connect…** waits until you connect. With several windows open, one window copies each SSH host and the others list that copy.
- An SSH host's sessions are copied one tool at a time, and each tool's sessions are listed as soon as they are copied. Meanwhile the host's heading shows how far the copy is, such as **2/6**, and its tooltip names the tool being copied. A host with nothing listed yet, such as one just added, says the same under its heading.
- Install **tmux on the remote host** so a session there keeps running when the connection drops or the tab closes. The app's bundled tmux is only for this Mac. Run `tmux -V` on the host to check installation, then select the session to reattach.
- When a tab's connection drops while its CLI runs in tmux, for example while your Mac sleeps with its lid closed, the tab says **Connection lost. Reconnecting…** and reattaches on its own once the host answers. It tries right after the drop, when your Mac wakes, when you come back to the app, and after **Connect…**, then less often for 15 minutes. If the CLI ended on the host, the tab stays ended and nothing restarts it; otherwise click **Reconnect** in the tab any time. The host itself must stay on: the CLI runs there, not on your Mac.
- Point at a host's heading and click its **⋯** for the host's own settings: **Connect…** (see [below](#hosts-that-ask-for-a-password-or-code)), **Disconnect** or **Connect** (see [below](#leave-a-host-alone)), **Sign-in command…** and **Sign in again…** (see [below](#sign-in-again-on-a-host)), **Forward a port…** and the host's port forwards (see [below](#forward-a-port-from-a-host)), **Use this host's tmux prefix** (see [terminal persistence](session-storage.md#terminal-persistence)), **Check this host…** (see [below](#when-something-fails-on-an-ssh-host)), and **Remove host**. The heading's right-click menu has them too, after **Add project…** and **Refresh**.
- If a session opens a shell instead of its CLI, see [shell startup](#shell-startup-on-ssh-hosts) below. If a refresh fails or a session won't start or resume, see [when something fails](#when-something-fails-on-an-ssh-host).

### Hosts that ask for a password or code

- When a host asks for a password, a key passphrase that isn't in your SSH agent, a one-time code, or another second factor, its refresh fails and its heading shows **Connect…** after its name. A host key this Mac hasn't seen does the same.
- Click **Connect…** and answer each of `ssh`'s questions in the sheet that opens. What `ssh` printed before a question, such as a sign-in banner or a second factor's options, is shown with it. A new host key is shown with its fingerprint; trust it only if it matches the host's.
- Once logged in, the host's refreshes, deletions, new sessions, and tabs all go through that one login, so nothing asks again. A tab opened while the host isn't logged in asks in its own terminal.
- The login stays open while JustSessions runs, even with nothing using it. It closes when the connection drops, as when the Mac sleeps, when you choose **Disconnect** from the host's **⋯** (see [below](#leave-a-host-alone)), when you remove the host, and when you quit JustSessions. After a drop, the next refresh offers **Connect…** again; the host's open tabs end, their CLIs keep running in tmux, and the tabs reconnect on their own once you connect again.
- JustSessions keeps nothing you type to log in.
- **Connect…** is also in the host's **⋯** when nothing asks, for example to share one login among several tabs on a host whose key asks for a tap each time.

### Leave a host alone

- Choose **Disconnect** from a host's **⋯** or right-click menu when JustSessions should stop reaching it, such as a host that is off, behind a VPN you left, or on a metered connection. From then on the app runs nothing on the host on its own: no refresh, check, sign-in check, or activity lookup, and no reconnecting of tabs. The host's sessions stay listed from the last copy, as this Mac's are from its disk, with none marked running, and its heading says **Disconnected**.
- Disconnecting closes the host's open tabs in every window. When one runs a CLI in tmux, it asks **Keep running** or **End sessions**, as closing several tabs does. The login made with **Connect…** and the host's port forwards end too.
- The host stays disconnected until you say otherwise, also after you quit and reopen JustSessions. Choose **Connect** from the same menu, click the heading's refresh button, or just use the host, such as resuming or starting a session there: it refreshes at once, and on its own from then on.

### Sign in again on a host

Some hosts need you to sign in again every so often after `ssh` has logged you in, for example to renew a credential that expires and asks for a PIN or a one-time code. JustSessions can run that command for you and ask its questions in a dialog.

- Choose **Sign-in command…** from the host's **⋯** or right-click menu, and type the command as you'd type it on the host, such as `kinit`. It runs there in your login shell, with a terminal, so it asks what it asks when you type it.
- To be asked only once the sign-in has expired, add a check command, such as `klist -s`, that exits with 0 while you're still signed in. JustSessions runs it every 5 minutes while one of its windows shows. Without one, you're asked every few hours, 10 unless you choose another, counted from your last sign-in through JustSessions.
- When a sign-in is due, the host's heading shows **Sign in…**. Its dialog opens by itself when you switch to JustSessions, or when JustSessions first finds the host due after it starts or after you save the command. Otherwise, while you're using JustSessions or it's in the background, you get a notification instead, so the dialog never catches typing meant for a terminal. Click **Sign in…** or the notification to sign in.
- The dialog shows what the command printed and asks the line it stopped on, such as `PIN:`. What you type goes to the command out of sight, then the next question follows, such as a code after the PIN. A security key that types its code can type it into the dialog. If the command asks the same question again, the dialog says your answer wasn't accepted.
- The dialog suits commands that ask one line at a time. A command that draws a full-screen interface, or opens a browser to sign in, needs a terminal: open one in a project on the host and run it there.
- If the command fails, an alert shows its last lines. **Cancel** stops the command, and the heading keeps **Sign in…**.
- **Sign in again…** in the host's menu signs in before it's due. Remove the command in the same sheet.
- The notification follows the **Needs my input** setting in **Settings > General**. JustSessions keeps the commands and when you last signed in, never what you type.

### Forward a port from a host

- To open a web app or another server running on a host, choose **Forward a port…** from the host's **⋯** or right-click menu. The sheet lists the ports the host listens on that `localhost` reaches, with the program's name when it's yours. Pick one, or type a port. The port on this Mac is the same unless you type another.
- The forward listens on `localhost` on this Mac only, so other computers can't use it, and reaches `localhost` on the host. It's listed in the host's menu, such as `localhost:3000 → 3000`, with **Open in browser**, **Copy address**, and **Stop forwarding**.
- If another program on this Mac uses the port, or the host doesn't allow forwarding, the sheet says so, with what `ssh` printed. On a host logged in with **Connect…**, a host that doesn't allow forwarding shows no error; the forward fails when you use it.
- A host's forwards share one connection, or go through its **Connect…** login, and ignore the port forwards in `~/.ssh/config`, so they don't clash with an open tab's. When the connection drops, for example while your Mac sleeps, they reconnect on their own.
- Forwards stop when you choose **Stop forwarding**, remove the host, choose **Disconnect** on the host, or quit JustSessions.

### Shell startup on SSH hosts

Each CLI on a host starts through your login shell, as `$SHELL -lic`, so it is found wherever your shell's startup files put it. If those files start another program in every interactive shell, such as tmux or zsh, that program's prompt shows instead of the CLI.

- JustSessions checks for this when you add a host, and once for hosts added before this check existed. It runs your shell in a terminal, as a tab does, and traces bash's or zsh's startup to find where it stopped. A refresh that fails, or can't tell which CLIs the host has, checks it again, once per run, and **Check this host…** in the host's **⋯** menu checks it any time. The menu also shows what the last check found.
- If a login shell without your interactive startup, `$SHELL -lc`, still runs the CLIs, the app starts everything that way on that host, and the host's **⋯** menu says so. Otherwise a warning shows beside the host's name; point at it to see the line where the startup stopped, or click it for the [user guide](https://yangzichao.github.io/JustSessions/guide.html#ssh-shell-startup).
- To fix it, make that line run only in interactive shells, and not in the ones JustSessions starts, which have `JUSTSESSIONS=1` set. Then choose **Check this host…** from the host's **⋯** menu:

  ```sh
  if [[ $- == *i* ]] && [ -z "$JUSTSESSIONS" ]; then exec zsh -l; fi
  ```

  Many hosts' bash, such as Fedora's and Debian's, also reads `~/.bashrc` for commands run over SSH, before JustSessions can set that variable, so checking for a terminal, `[ -t 1 ]`, isn't enough. A line that starts another program there blocks the host whichever way the app starts the shell.

- Without your interactive startup, a CLI it puts on your `PATH`, as nvm's setup does, is missing from **New session**. Move that setup to `~/.profile`, or above the line in `~/.bashrc` that stops for non-interactive shells.
- The check lets the startup run as a tab would, so what it starts, such as a tmux session, can stay running on the host.

### When something fails on an SSH host

A refresh, a new session, or a resume on a host runs programs installed there, such as `rsync`, tmux, and the CLI, through your shell's startup files. When one of them fails, JustSessions can show which.

- When a CLI fails as it starts, its tab ends showing what the CLI printed, and the bar above it shows its exit status, such as **Exited (127)**. On a host whose tmux is older than 3.2, tmux clears that output and the tab shows only `[exited]`.
- On an SSH host, when the tab ended with an error within 30 seconds of starting, or the host has a warning beside its name, the bar says so, with that warning, and **Check host** is its main button.
- Choose **Check this host…** from the host's **⋯** menu, or click the warning beside its name after a failed refresh. When a host's tab ends, as when a session fails to resume, click **Check host** above its terminal; that check also looks at the tab's folder and its CLI.
- The check logs in as the app does, checks your [shell startup](#shell-startup-on-ssh-hosts), then looks up `rsync`, tmux, each CLI, and, for OpenCode and Antigravity, `python3` with its `sqlite3` module. It asks each CLI for its version, for at most 10 seconds. It only reads, and changes nothing on the host. While it runs, the sheet lists these steps and how long the current one has taken, so a slow host shows where it is slow.
- Problems come first, then warnings. Each shows what the host printed and a command to run in Terminal. If that command fails the same way there, the problem is on the host: fix it there, or send it to whoever runs the host. **Copy report** copies the findings as text, starting with this Mac's JustSessions, macOS, and OpenSSH versions, for an issue or a message. **Check again** runs it again after you change something. A check from an ended tab also keeps the last lines the tab showed, such as tmux's error, at the end of the report.
- JustSessions also checks each host on its own while a window shows on screen: once after each app update, once a day, and soon after a tab of the host ends with an error right after it starts. These checks don't trace your shell startup again; they use what its last check found. Each refresh also notices when the host's tmux can't reach its running tmux server. Either way, a warning shows beside the host's name; point at it to see the problem, or click it for the report.
- **Fix with agent…**, the report's main button when it found something to fix, starts a CLI session whose first message is the report, how JustSessions runs things on the host, and the parts of this guide about what the check found. Pick where it runs, on the host or on this Mac, from where it reaches the host with `ssh`, and which CLI, and edit the message before you start it. The agent runs outside tmux, so a broken tmux can't end it, and it's told to ask you before it ends tmux sessions, stops processes, changes packages, or edits your startup files.
- A CLI found on the host but failing to print its version, as when it is a Node program and `node` isn't on the login shell's `PATH`, fails to start sessions too.
- tmux on the host needs version 2.6 or later. A tmux server started by another tmux version than the one on your `PATH`, as after upgrading tmux while sessions run, can't be reached: its sessions keep running but can't be opened, and a tab that tries ends at once with an error. Reattach them with the tmux that started the server, or end them with it and start them again.

## Clean up

- Delete sessions one at a time, in a multi-selection (⌘-click, ⇧-click), or per project, always after a confirmation. The one exception is a session started with **Delete the session when its tab closes**: its box was the confirmation.
- The bottom of the sidebar shows the progress of deleting several sessions, for example **Deleting 37 of 140…**. Click **Cancel** to stop after the session being deleted; the rest stay listed and are not reported as failures. A session on an SSH host can take up to a minute to finish or time out, so stopping can take that long.
- You can keep deleting while a deletion runs. The new sessions join it: it deletes them after the others, the progress bar counts them, and one alert at the end covers them all. Cancel stops those too. Sessions you delete after choosing Cancel are deleted once it has stopped.
- Refreshing a host waits until a deletion from it ends. Sessions deleted while their host is refreshing wait for the refresh, then are deleted.
- If an SSH host can't be reached or doesn't respond within a minute during a deletion, the app skips its remaining sessions and keep them listed, and still delete the other sessions. A connection that stops responding, for example after the Mac sleeps, is given up after about 30 seconds.
- When sessions can't be deleted, the app says how many were deleted, why the others weren't, and what to do, for example to check your network or VPN, or to click **Connect…** on the host's heading. A host's connection problem is named once instead of for each session. If the cause may pass, such as a lost connection, click **Try Again** to delete the sessions still listed on the hosts that had it; the alert says which. Sessions that failed for another reason, or that Cancel left, are not retried. If a deletion runs, the retry joins it; if a host the sessions are on is being refreshed, the retry starts when the refresh ends.
- A session that is already gone, for example because it was deleted just before the connection dropped, counts as deleted: it leaves the list without an error.
- Projects stay in the sidebar after their last session is deleted, including after restarting the app. Choose **Archive project** from a project's **⋯** or right-click menu to hide it without deleting its sessions or folder. While a host has archived projects, its heading's **+**, such as on **This Mac**, opens a menu: choose **Archived projects (…)** to list them with a **Restore** button each, or **Add project…** to add a folder. The heading's right-click menu has the same item. Starting a new session in an archived folder also restores it.
- Select several projects with ⌘-click, or select a continuous range with Shift-click, then click **Archive** at the bottom of the sidebar or choose **Archive … projects** from a selected project's **⋯** or right-click menu. Open terminals keep running. To also delete their sessions, click **Archive and delete sessions…** or choose **Archive … projects and delete all sessions (…)…**, then confirm. Sessions that still run have their tabs closed and their CLIs ended first; a session that can't be deleted stays on disk and comes back if its project is restored. Use a project's arrow to expand or collapse it while keeping the selection.
- Click empty space in the sidebar or press Escape while the sidebar is focused to cancel a project or session batch selection. A single session's conversation stays open.
- Claude Code, Antigravity, and Pi sessions on this Mac go to the macOS Trash; a Pi session's folder beside its file goes too, if there is one. Codex uses `codex delete --force`, Kiro CLI uses `kiro-cli chat --delete-session <session-id>`, and OpenCode uses `opencode session delete <session-id>`, which also deletes the session's subagent sessions. SSH hosts have no Trash, so deletions there are permanent.
- For a session with an open terminal tab, in any window, or still running in tmux, a session's right-click or **⋯** menu offers **Close and delete session…** instead. After you confirm, it closes the tab, ends the CLI, waits up to 5 seconds for the CLI to exit, then deletes the session. Deleting a multi-selection or a project's sessions does the same for each of its sessions that runs, then deletes them all; the confirmation says how many it ends first.

## Settings

Open **Settings** at the bottom of the sidebar, or press ⌘,.

- **General > Interface language**: **Follow System** by default, or choose **English** or **Chinese**. Switching updates the interface in place and preserves running terminal tabs. System languages without a matching translation fall back to English.
- **General** also sets whether tabs reopen at launch, what closing a tab and ⌘-clicking a file in a terminal do, and which [notifications](#notifications) to send. Open, closed, and selected tabs are saved as they change, including sessions newly linked to a tab. Recovery does not depend on a normal app quit; tabs waiting for an unavailable host or CLI remain in the saved list until they can reopen. Closing the last window keeps its tabs, so they reopen when you click the Dock icon or launch the app again; closing one of several windows discards that window's tabs.
- **Appearance > App**: choose **System** to switch between light and dark with your Mac, or keep the app **Light** or **Dark**, and choose its theme: JustSessions, GitHub, Atom One, Tokyo Night, Catppuccin, or Gruvbox. Each theme has light and dark colors, and the app's appearance picks between them. Choosing a theme gives terminals its colors too, even if they had a color scheme of their own.
- **Appearance > App > Interface size**: makes the app's own text and controls larger or smaller, from 90% to 150%: the sidebar, tabs, preview, sheets, and Settings, in every window as you drag. Terminals and the conversation's text keep their own sizes, which ⌘+ and ⌘− change. The sidebar widens with its text above 100%, so its rows show as much of their titles.
- **Appearance > App > Customize colors**: change the chosen theme's background, sidebar, fields and buttons, your messages, and text and accents, in its light or dark version. Changes apply as you pick them and stay with that theme; **Reset** gives it back its own colors. Text is made readable on your colors, so the text color can come out darker or lighter than you chose. A background, sidebar, field, or message color that text can't stay readable on is refused; any text color, and going back to the theme's own color, is taken. Terminals that match the app theme take its background.
- **Appearance > Terminal > Engine**: new tabs open in SwiftTerm's terminal. Choose **Ghostty** to open them in [Ghostty](https://ghostty.org)'s terminal instead, which draws on the GPU and uses less of the Mac's main processor while a CLI redraws, and choose **SwiftTerm** again if something looks or behaves differently. The choice applies to tabs opened after you change it; open tabs, and tabs that reconnect or move to another window, keep the engine they opened with. Tabs reopened at launch open with the engine chosen then.
- **Appearance > Terminal > Colors**: terminals use the app theme's colors unless you pick a color scheme of their own: one of the app themes, Dracula, or Nord. **Import** copies the colors of iTerm2's default profile, or of an iTerm2 color preset (`.itermcolors`) file. JustSessions reads iTerm2's settings only when you choose that, and never again after.
- **Appearance > Terminal > Light or dark**: terminals match the app unless you give them the **Light** or **Dark** version of their colors. A scheme with only one version, such as Dracula or Nord, keeps it. Changes apply to open terminals right away. A CLI that asks the terminal for its background then gets the new one, also through tmux 3.6 or later; on an SSH host with an older tmux, such as Ubuntu 24.04's 3.4, the CLI keeps the colors it started with. Terminals change text that would be hard to read on their background, so text a CLI colored for the other brightness stays readable: SwiftTerm darkens or lightens it just enough, and Ghostty draws it in black or white. Block characters, such as pixel art, keep their colors.
- **Appearance > Terminal > Color intensity**: makes the 16 colors that shells and many CLIs pick from stronger or softer, from 50% to 200%. At 100%, the default, they're the color scheme's own. Each color keeps its brightness, so text stays as easy to read; very light colors can grow only a little stronger. Other colors, such as most of Claude Code's, stay as they are. Open terminals change once you stop dragging.
- **Appearance > Terminal > Font**: **System Monospaced**, or any monospaced font installed on your Mac, including Nerd Fonts such as JetBrainsMono Nerd Font Mono. Fonts whose letters don't fit a terminal's cells are left out, such as Osaka and Lantinghei TC: macOS calls them monospaced, but terminals would draw their letters far apart. With any font but System Monospaced, Nerd Font icons, such as the Powerline separators and file icons of shell prompts, show even when the font lacks them: JustSessions includes Symbols Nerd Font Mono for them. Ghostty also shows them with System Monospaced. In SwiftTerm, macOS doesn't let its system font use another font for missing characters, so System Monospaced shows Nerd Font icons only from a Nerd Font installed on your Mac. A font you remove shows as not installed, and one you chose before it was left out shows as not supported; either way, terminals use System Monospaced until you choose another. Ghostty draws your font as SwiftTerm does, at the same size and weight, in cells of the same size, and with the same fonts for characters it lacks, such as Chinese. Wide characters, such as Chinese, sit a point or two further left in their cells in Ghostty.
- **Permissions**: whether macOS allows notifications and, optionally, Full Disk Access, which lets CLIs in terminals open files in protected folders such as Documents without macOS asking about each one, and, also optionally, Accessibility, which lets CLIs in terminals control apps, such as clicking a menu or typing keys through System Events with `osascript`. macOS gives Accessibility to every CLI in a terminal, so allow it only if you want them to have it. On macOS 15 and later it also lists Local Network, which JustSessions and its CLIs need to reach a device on your network, such as an SSH host at a local address. macOS doesn't let apps check that one, so look for JustSessions in **System Settings > Privacy & Security > Local Network**. The tab checks again when you come back from System Settings, and checking never shows a prompt.
